Slow drains everywhereOne slow sink is a blockage. Every drain slowing down at once points at the tank.

Wet, lush patchesSoggy ground or unusually green grass over the drainfield, even in dry weather.
BackupsWater backing up into the lowest drains in the house when you flush or run laundry.
It's been 3–5 yearsMost household tanks need pumping on that cycle. Longer gaps risk the drainfield.

How a septic system works

Three parts, one job: take everything that leaves your house, separate it, treat it, and return clean water to the ground. Here's each stage.

System_Diagram.bmp –□×
1

Everything flows in

Every drain, toilet and appliance sends wastewater through one main line into the buried tank.

2

The tank separates

Solids sink as sludge, oils float as scum, and the clearer middle layer, effluent, flows on. Bacteria in the tank break down much of the waste.

3

Effluent flows out

Baffles hold the scum and sludge back so only the liquid layer leaves for the drainfield. This is why pumping matters, a full tank stops separating.

4

Soil finishes it

Perforated pipes spread the liquid through the drainfield, where the soil filters and treats it before it rejoins the groundwater.

Septic-safe habits

  • Spread laundry across the week instead of all in one day
  • Fix leaking taps and running toilets promptly
  • Keep a service record, dates, pumping, inspections
  • Keep heavy vehicles off the tank and drainfield
  • Direct roof and surface water away from the drainfield

Never put these down the drain

  • Wipes of any kind, including ones labelled “flushable”
  • Fats, oils, grease and coffee grounds
  • Paint, solvents, fuel and harsh chemicals
  • Sanitary products, nappies, cotton buds, dental floss
  • Large volumes of bleach or antibacterial cleaners at once

Frequently asked questions

The questions every septic owner asks eventually, answered straight, so you're informed before you ever need to book.

For most households, every 3 to 5 years. The real interval depends on tank size, how many people live in the house, and how much water and solids the system handles. A garbage disposal, for example, fills a tank noticeably faster. The estimator on the “How Septic Works” page gives you a starting point; an inspection gives you the exact answer.
Sludge and scum build up until the tank can no longer separate them. Solids then escape into the drainfield and clog the soil, and a failed drainfield is the most expensive part of the system to put right. Pumping is cheap insurance by comparison.
Yes, but it adds ground food solids to the tank, which means the sludge layer builds faster and pumping needs to happen more often. Scrape plates into the bin or compost where you can, and treat the disposal as occasional, not a bin.
A healthy tank already contains the bacteria it needs, supplied by normal household use. Additives are generally not a substitute for pumping, and some products can do more harm than good. If a seller claims an additive means you'll never need pumping again, that claim alone tells you what you need to know.
Get an independent inspection before you complete, where the tank is, its size and condition, when it was last pumped, and whether the drainfield is performing. Ask the seller for any service records. A system is a significant asset with significant replacement costs, so treat it like the survey treats the roof.
A slightly lusher patch can be normal, the field is returning treated water and nutrients to the soil. But very lush growth combined with soggy ground, smells, or slow drains can mean the field is overloaded or failing, and that's worth an inspection.
With correct installation and regular maintenance, tanks commonly last for decades, and a well-cared-for drainfield can serve 20–30 years or more. The biggest life-shorteners are neglected pumping, overloading with water, vehicles driving over the field, and the wrong things going down the drains.
Keep it to grass. Tree and shrub roots seek out the moisture and can invade and block pipes, and buildings, patios, pools or driveways over the field prevent it breathing and make any future repair far harder. Keep the area clear and accessible.
