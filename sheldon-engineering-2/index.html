Emergency help

 
Sewage backing up, overflowing, or strong sewage smells indoors? Treat it as urgent. Do these things first, then get in touch.

 

 CALL: (424) 425-1228

Do this now
  • Stop using water, no flushing, showers, laundry, or dishwasher until it's checked.
  • Keep people and pets away from any backed-up or wet area.
  • Turn off power to affected areas if water is near electrics, only if you can do it safely.
  • Note what happened: when it started, which drains are affected, any recent heavy rain.
  • If you know it, locate your tank lid / access point so the crew can get straight in.

 
Don't do this
  • Don't pour drain chemicals or “septic shock” additives into a backup.
  • Don't open the tank yourself, gases inside a tank are dangerous.
  • Don't keep flushing to “clear it”, it usually makes the backup worse.
  • Don't ignore gurgling, slow drains everywhere, or sewage smells, they're early warnings, not quirks.

The 5 things that move any septic price

 

 
1. Tank size
A 1,500-gallon tank simply takes longer and holds more than a 750.

 
2. Depth & access
Buried lids, tight gates, and long hose runs add time. Clear access keeps it simple.

 
3. Distance
Travel to the property and to disposal is part of every operator's cost.

 
4. Condition
Thick solids, damage, or a neglected tank can mean extra work on the day.

 
5. Timing
Planned routine visits are the cheapest route. Emergencies cost more, another reason the schedule tool exists.
